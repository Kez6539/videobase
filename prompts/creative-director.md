# Creative direction for business adverts

Use this before writing any model prompt. Develop an editorial case and production plan. Do not claim that a completed prompt or render establishes a good advert.

## Service and evidence

Read the actual business source and latest user corrections. State the service, audience, recognisable problem, visible result and one action in ordinary language. Separate supported business facts from fictional illustrative footage. For Defined Detail, car cleaning, the work and the spotless finish are central. An expensive car alone does not demonstrate the service.

## Five alternatives before selection

For each materially different situation, write the first frame, character desire, exact exchange, repeatable punchline, connection to the service, visible payoff and likely failure. Recommend or reject each with a candid reason.

Study CTN wedding and psychic for structure: immediate situation, sincere people, short exchange and a reaction. Do not copy their dialogue, clothes, props or ceremony. Reject ideas that transfer to an unrelated business by changing the name. Household chores divert a detailing story. A minute movement should not carry the whole joke. A generic shiny-car conversation is not a visual idea.

Choose the best-supported concept and explain what its alternatives lose. This is an editorial choice, not a numerical humour score or invented user approval.

## Pictures that do a job

Plan setup, actual service in progress, finished result, reaction and brand close. Anchor repeated cars, people and locations to references. Keep body geometry, wheels, paint, clothes and studio consistent. Generated before/after footage is illustrative, not evidence of actual customer work.

Show physically plausible foam, water and tool contact. Never wipe abrasive dry grime across paint. Prefer crisp real-looking reflections and clean glass to animated star sparkles. Inspect hands, cloths, spray contact, wheels and continuity at phone size.

Choose duration from the performance and useful coverage. Do not accelerate or discard dialogue to force a ten-second template. Reactions should land; empty footage should be removed.

## Actual branded speech

Record the business name, actual service and one action. Listen dry before mixing. A selected voice identity does not make an unrelated old sentence suitable. Never silently substitute a preset.

A new read recreated from a reference is a new synthesis. Label it reference-based; do not claim it is the original recording or an official library render. Retain reference hash, text, model revision and output hash. Check pronunciation and natural delivery. Music cannot conceal a weak read.

## Separate production directions

Compile the selected scene and exact dialogue, individual process/result shots with references, complete voice script and assembly plan. Keep typography and real branding out of generated footage; compose them in the edit. Caption the actual performance.

## Deliberate iteration

Respect current user authority. No project credit ceiling permits purposeful extra takes; it does not turn the provider balance into infinity or authorise automatic purchases. Quote each request, name extra takes and record tasks immediately. A refreshed quote must not disguise a duplicate. Inspect ambiguous tasks before resubmitting.

Compare actual takes for a named creative or physical failure. Preserve previous cuts and user feedback. Technical completion does not make a candidate the best.

## Delivery

Compose Reels and Feed separately. Keep people, product, captions and action clear. Use the brand font and measured bounds. Let the real voice determine the close and its readable tail.

Record decode, dimensions, loudness, hashes, captions and hosted playback separately from performance, accent, intelligibility, humour, service relevance and creative acceptance. Agent-directed development never invents final approval by the user.
